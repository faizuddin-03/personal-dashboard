# Flow 2 — Closing your week

**Who:** Everyone
**When:** Once a week, on your last working day.

This covers the part of the record that a list of time entries can't show on its own.

---

## Step 1 — Write the wrap-up

Go to the **Week wrap-up** tab on My timesheet. There are four questions for you to answer, plus two things that fill themselves in.

You answer these four:

- **Key achievements** — what actually got finished this week?
- **Additional blockers** — anything that slowed you down that isn't already listed above
- **Support needed next week** — what do you need, and who do you need it from?
- **Proposed priorities next week** — what should next week start with?

These two are already there:

- **Blockers raised this week** — this fills in automatically from any entry you ticked as Blocked during the week. You don't need to retype them.
- **Friday AI contribution** — what you **presented or shared** at the Friday AI session, with a link if you have one. This is about contributing something, not about attending. Turning up to the session, or building AI tooling during the week, does not fill it — so it can read `Missing` on a week where you did plenty of AI work. (Charmain's reading, 30 Aug 2026; worth confirming with May Chin.)

This is your own account of the week, and it's the first thing your lead reads before making any decision about it.

**Things worth knowing:**

- **It's due on your last working day,** even though the week itself doesn't close until Sunday.
- **Once you submit it, you can't edit it any more.** Submitting hands the week over to your lead. If you need to change something after that, they have to reopen it, and the reopen is recorded.

---

## Step 2 — Answer the weekly pulse and submit

There are two more questions, one click each:

- **Buddy / reporting-line communication** — did you speak to your assigned buddy or your reporting line this week? Options: Not applicable, Not needed, Once, 2–3 times, more than 3 times.
- **Alignment with your JD and general expectations** — did this week's work and priorities match your job responsibilities? Options: Fully aligned, Mostly aligned, Partially aligned, Not aligned.

These are read alongside your hours, not instead of them.

Then click **Submit wrap-up**. If you're not finished, click **Save draft** instead. Submitting hands the week to your lead and locks it until they reopen it.

**Things worth knowing:**

- **If you were on leave for the whole week, you don't need to submit a wrap-up,** and it isn't counted as missed.

## Step 3 — Record any night work, and say if you started late

If you did a deployment or got called out **outside working hours**, it goes on this card — not in your timesheet.

Those hours never count as work. The reason is that putting a 1am call-out into your 40-hour week would make it look like you had more capacity than you did, and would effectively reward being paged.

Each row records:

- **Type** — deployment, or production support / issue
- **Date and start time** — for example, Thu 6 Aug, 1:00 AM
- **Duration** — for example, 5h 30m
- **Started late?** — for example, half a day (4h) on Thu 6, or leave it blank

What the night earns depends on which of three situations it was (see the list below). The entry then asks whether you came in later the next working day because of it.

Once recorded, the call-out shows up on your lead's review and in the monthly reports, and it never inflates your week.

**Things worth knowing:**

- **A production deployment earns a hardship allowance** if it ran an hour or more. How much it pays depends on how long it ran. Those bands are in the employee handbook, not in this tool.
- **On-call and production support are the CTO's decision.** They might earn the allowance and they might not. Record it either way. There's no on-call roster yet, so for now each case is decided individually.
- **Working late on your own work isn't hardship.** That's extra work, and it has its own card just below this one. It earns no allowance and no late start.
- **A late start needs the CTO's approval.** Agree it with them first, then mark it on the entry. Marking it takes those hours off the next day straight away, so you're never measured against a morning you were told to take. The CTO then approves or rejects it afterwards.
- **If the CTO rejects the late start, that day goes back to expecting a full day** — and your declaration stays on the record. That's why you agree it first.
- **Leaving "Started late" blank is the normal answer.** Plenty of people work the deployment and then work their usual day.
- **You can set the late start later, on the row itself.** That's usually when you actually know: you record the deployment at 11pm and only find out the next morning.
- **The late start lands on the working day after the night.** So a Friday night release has nowhere to put one, because the next day is Saturday.

## Step 4 — Declare extra work you did in your own time

This card sits just under night work. Use it when you worked an evening or a weekend **on your own initiative** — nobody paged you, nobody asked you to.

You record three things: how long it took, why it had to be outside working hours, and a ticket or link if there is one.

Going the extra mile does get noticed, and this card is the only place the company can see it. Your lead acknowledges each declaration, and if there's a real pattern of it, the CTO can credit it on the merit ladder when your month is rated.

**Things worth knowing:**

- **It never counts as work.** It isn't billable, it doesn't top up your 40-hour week, and it earns no hardship allowance and no late start. What it earns is being read.
- **It sits on top of a full day, not instead of one.** Your 8 hours are where the work is meant to get done, because that's when the people who can unblock you are around. An evening at home can't make up for an afternoon that went quietly.
- **The better way to get more out of a week is to work faster, not longer.** Use AI heavily and daily — on the code, the tests, the analysis, the write-ups — so that what used to take a day takes an afternoon. Shipping sooner is worth more than staying later.
- **You can work past your hours if you want to, but only after a full day.** A tight deadline, a rush job, or just wanting to be one of the strongest performers in Group IT are all fair reasons. The one condition is that your normal working hours were already used at full capacity.
- **Don't declare time you spent teaching yourself.** Self-learning in the evenings and at weekends is encouraged, especially for juniors, but keep it off this card. Learning isn't delivery, and there's no honest way to measure it.
- **Write what you did and why, not just a number of hours.** Eight hours on a Sunday with no explanation behind it is exactly the kind of claim this card exists to catch.
- **If you change the hours or the date, it becomes a new claim** and your lead has to acknowledge it again. Just rewording the description doesn't.
- **If your lead disputes a declaration, it stays on your record** along with the reason they gave, so the disagreement is written down rather than lost.
