[button] What factors to take into consideration in choosing policy that is suitable for me? + In choosing a policy that well-suits your need, there are many aspects to take into consideration. Among all, you may want to look for the following:
Coverage Type
For motor insurance, there are three main coverage types. Each and every coverage type provides different forms of compensations to you. The three coverage types are:

1. Third Party cover
This policy insures you against claims for bodily injuries or deaths caused to other persons (known as the third party), as well as loss or damage to third party property caused by your car.
1. Third Party, Fire and Theft cover
This policy insures you against claims for third party bodily injury and death, third party property loss or damage, and loss or damage to your own car due to accidental fire or theft.
1. Comprehensive cover
This policy provides the widest coverage among these three. i.e. third party bodily injury and death, third party property loss or damage and loss or damage to your own car due to accidental fire, theft or an accident.
Premium Amount
Your premium is the amount of money you pay to an insurance company to provide insurance on your car. Policy with low premium undeniably lead to cost-saving, yet that option may compromise some protection that you may need for your car. On the other hand, opting for policy with high premium too may not neccessarily be a wise move for you. Always consider your financial circumstances and the type of protection that your car truly needs when making choices related to your car insurance as what may work for some may not be ideal for others.
Insurer's Credibility
Another important factor is to check insurers' credibility before proceeding with plan purchase. This research will assist you in understanding the reliability of the company, which is paramount, particularly if the needs to make claim settlement arises.
Exclusions
Exclusions are specific factors/situations that are not covered under insurance plan. It is important to go through the policy documents well in advance before buying one, to avoid any unpleasant surprise during the claim process.

[button] What is the importance of insuring my car at the appropriate market value? + The importance of insuring the car at the correct market value cannot be overstated. If your car is under-insured, you run the risk of getting penalised. The insurer will only bear part of the loss in proportion to the difference between the market value and the sum insured. Kindly refer to the following illustration:
Car market value is RM60,000. Sum Insured is RM50,000.
Assuming your car involved in an accident which resulted in losses worth RM20,000, what is highest amount of compensation that you may attain?
Loss payable by insurer
Sum insured / Car market value x loss
RM50,000/RM60,000 x RM20,000
RM16,666.67
The balance of RM3,333.33 (RM20,000 - RM16,666.67) will need to be borne by you.
*Do keep in mind that the above illustration is a general illustration only.
On the contrary, over-insuring your car would be a waste of money as the insurer will compensate you up till the market value of your car only.

[button] What is No Claim Discount (NCD)? + No Claim Discount (NCD) is a discount for drivers who have not made any insurance claims during the preceding years of the insured period. If you didn’t file any claims over the years, your NCD discount will increase. However, once you make a claim, your NCD will go to 0% again and after 1 year of paying your premium to full, you start building your NCD again.
The amount of NCD you may receive depends on the type of vehicle, coverage and the number of years you have not filed a claim. Normally private cars can go up to 55% of NCD.

[button] What are market value and agreed value coverages? + Market value refers to the current calculated worth of your car.
If you choose the market value coverage, your car would be covered for what it is currently worth in the market, and you will be compensated for your car theft or loss based on the market value of your car at the time of theft or loss, rather than market value at the time of your insurance renewal.
Agreed value refers to the value agreed by both the insurer and policyholder at the time of insurance renewal.
Insurers determine the agreed value based on a few underwriting factors and risk assessment. Say that you choose the agreed value as the sum insured for your car, your insurer will compensate you exactly as the agreed value.
Refer to our example below:
You have insured your car at RM60,000 in January this year. In September, you would need to make claim on a total loss or theft of your car. In the instance where your car is valued at RM58,000 in September, if you chose:

1. market value coverage, your compensation pay-out would be based on RM58,000 rather than RM60,000.
1. agreed value coverage, your compensation pay-out would be based on RM60,000.
