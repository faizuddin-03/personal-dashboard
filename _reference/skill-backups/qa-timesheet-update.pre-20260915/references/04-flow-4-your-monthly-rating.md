# Flow 4 — Your monthly rating

**Who:** Everyone
**When:** Once a month. Your lead writes it, the CTO approves it, and you only see it after it's been approved.

There's nothing for you to do in this flow. It's here so you know what the card says and how the number was arrived at.

---

## Step 1 — What the card says

The card is the rubric for your role. Each criterion is scored **1 to 5**, and for each one there are five written descriptions of what that level looks like in your role. Your lead picks the description that matches, and the number follows from that.

Every criterion on your card shows three things: the criterion name, the wording it was scored against, and a line of comment from your lead. So the number is never the whole message — you can always see what it was based on.

At the bottom is the **summary for the month**, which is your lead's paragraph on what the scores add up to.

The card shows your average and the band it falls into:

| Band | Average |
|---|---|
| Below expectations | under 2.5 |
| Meets expectations | 2.5 – 3.5 |
| Exceeds expectations | 3.5 – 4.5 |
| Outstanding | above 4.5 |

A criterion that genuinely didn't apply that month is marked **N/A** and left out of the average, rather than counted as a zero.

The card also records which version of your role's rubric was live when it was opened. That way, if the rubric changes later, it doesn't retroactively move an old month's score.

**Things worth knowing:**

- **Nothing reaches you until the CTO approves it.** A score that's still being discussed never appears on your screen.
- **Once it's approved, nothing on it changes.** The card is closed at whatever figure it was approved at.
- **There's no reply box on a rating, and that's deliberate.** If you disagree with something, that's a conversation to have with your lead, not a comment to type.

---

## Step 2 — What can move the number

Two things sit outside the 1–5 criteria and can push your rating up or down. Both are the CTO's to record and to price, and the card shows you the arithmetic rather than just the final figure — for example, `3.86 + 0.50 merit` giving `4.36`.

There are three tables at the foot of the card:

**Production crises caused this month.** Incidents in production that you caused or contributed to. "None" is the usual answer. These are recorded rather than scored — the incident speaks for itself, and what it should really affect is the Verification and Ownership criteria higher up the card.

**Systems rebuilt and rolled out.** A third-party system you rebuilt in-house and actually put in front of its users this month. The row records what shipped, whether you did it alone or with a team, and how it landed with the people now using it. This one adds to your rating.

**Conduct and professionalism.** How you were to work with that month, in cases where it cost the team something. "None" is the usual answer. The CTO records each one and picks the reason from a fixed list, and each reason carries its own cost.

**Things worth knowing:**

- **The full price lists are published,** and they're the same for everybody. They're worth reading before something happens rather than after. (See *Reference — What a merit or demerit is worth*, at the end of Flow 6.)
- **A row that hasn't been graded is worth nothing either way.** It can't be left hanging unpriced on an approved card.
