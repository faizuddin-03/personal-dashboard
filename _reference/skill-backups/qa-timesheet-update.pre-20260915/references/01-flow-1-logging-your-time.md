# Flow 1 — Logging your time

**Who:** Everyone
**When:** Every working day, before 11:00 PM. This is the whole daily job — nothing else is asked of you day to day.

---

## Step 1 — Open My timesheet and pick the day

Click **My timesheet** in the sidebar and it opens on today. Along the top there's a strip of day cards, so you can go back to earlier days in the current week. A day stays open for logging until **11:00 PM Malaysia time**, and after that it's locked for good. You type your entries into the panel on the right-hand side, which opens with the day already selected. A banner at the top counts down to the 11:00 PM cut-off.

**Things worth knowing:**

- **If you miss a day, you can't go back and fill it in yourself.** You have to ask your lead to reopen it. Once they do, you get a short deadline to finish it and hand it back.
- **You don't owe anything for dates before you joined.** Days before your timesheet started aren't counted as missing — nothing was ever expected there.
- **Only log your actual working hours.** Your 8 hours run from whenever you start, so if you start at 8am your day ends at 5pm. Carrying on into the evening is welcome, but that's your own effort — don't log it, and it's never billable. If you want that effort to be seen, declare it as **extra work** on the week tab (see Flow 2, Step 4).

---

## Step 2 — Say whether the hour is billable, and which kind

First pick **Billable** or **Non-billable**. If it's billable, you then pick one of two kinds:

- **Delivery** — actually building the thing: coding, testing, designing, writing documentation. Attach the ticket if there is one. If there's no ticket, write in the notes what the work was.
- **Meeting** — any call, workshop or walkthrough about a client's project, whether or not the client is in the room. You don't need a ticket, but you do need to say what the meeting was about.

Both are charged to the client. The reason for splitting them is so the company can tell the difference between a month spent building and a month spent in meetings.

**Things worth knowing:**

- **A meeting needs a project, not a ticket.** Attaching a project is what makes a client call loggable at all, and you always have to write a note saying what the call was about.
- **Delivery needs either a ticket or notes.** If you give neither, the entry won't save. And either way, your lead will see a "No ticket" badge on it.
- **Non-billable work doesn't take a project.** You pick a category instead.
- **If you're not sure which side something belongs to,** check the Guidelines page. It has the rule plus worked examples.

---

## Step 3 — Say who else was there

This field shows up on every non-billable entry and on billable meetings. The idea is simple: if someone else was in that hour with you, both timesheets should say the same thing.

To use it, type **@** and pick the person from the list. It only accepts real accounts, not free text, so nobody gets named by accident through a typo. The people you name appear as chips, and you remove one with a single click.

It's recommended, not compulsory. Name people for something like a standup or a training session. Don't bother if you spent the hour writing documentation on your own.

**Things worth knowing:**

- **Colleagues can name you too.** The *Named in* panel at the top of your timesheet shows every time somebody else logged you into their hour that week.
- **That panel is read-only, and that's deliberate.** Somebody naming you doesn't change your hours at all. If they got a day wrong, the fix is to go and tell them.
- **Your lead can see whether the two logs match up.** It's meant as a prompt to go and ask, not as a judgement on anyone.

---

## Step 4 — Fill in the duration, and flag anything blocked

You can type the duration however you'd normally say it — "1h 30m", "90" and "1.5h" all work. There are also quick buttons for the common amounts.

Tick **Blocked on this task** if something is holding the work up. Blocked entries get collected into your weekly blocker summary, which is how the company tells the difference between a month spent waiting on other people and a month spent doing nothing.

**Things worth knowing:**

- **An incident during working hours is just a normal entry.** Log it as billable time on that project, the same as any other hour.
- **Tick Blocked for a meeting that keeps dragging on, too.** Blocked isn't only for waiting on someone else. An afternoon that got eaten by a meeting is a delivery risk, and your lead should see it.
- **Don't log out-of-hours firefighting here at all.** If you got woken at 1am, that goes in as night work on the week tab. It never counts towards the 40-hour week, and it's paid as a hardship allowance instead (see Flow 2, Step 3).

---

## Step 5 — Mark leave when you're away

Leave isn't a time entry. Use the **Mark leave** button next to the week stepper. It opens a drawer where you choose:

- the type — annual, emergency, medical, compassionate, paternity or maternity
- the portion — **Full**, **½ AM** or **½ PM**. A half day says *which* half, so pick the right one;
  half days apply to a single date
- the dates — one date or a range. **There is no note field** (verified 3 Sep 2026), so the type and
  the portion are the whole record

The number of working days it covers is shown under the calendar. Leave replaces the hours you would have logged, so a leave day never shows up as a missed day, and your capacity for that week drops to match.

**A public holiday behaves differently, and the app gets it wrong.** It is flagged `Missed` like any other empty day (verified 25 Aug 2026: `TUE 25 Missed`, week reading `32h / 40h`). Read the week against the days you actually worked instead — a 4 day week is **32h of 32h**, and the billable target scales from 30h to **24h**. Do not ask for the day to be reopened; that would mean logging hours on a holiday. Ask your lead to correct the flag.

**Things worth knowing:**

- **A half day covers one date and half of it.** The other half is a normal working half-day, and it still expects entries.
- **To file the other half of a half day, pick ½ AM or ½ PM first.** A date that already holds leave is greyed out in the calendar, so choosing the portion before the date is what makes it selectable again.
- **You can't edit a leave record.** To change one, delete it and file it again.
- **If you're on leave for a whole week, you don't need to write a wrap-up,** and nothing about that week counts as missed.
- **Filing leave records it, but it doesn't approve it.** Agree the leave with your lead first, the usual way.
